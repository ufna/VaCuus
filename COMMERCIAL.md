# Commercial licensing — who needs to buy, and how

The public source of VaCuus is licensed under the **Business Source License 1.1**
(`LICENSE.md`). In practice:

## Free, for everyone, right now

- **Reading, studying, forking, modifying** the source — the BUSL grant covers copying,
  modification, derivative works and redistribution.
- **All non-production use**: prototypes, evaluation, internal experiments, learning
  projects, game jams that stay unreleased.
- **Noncommercial production use** (the Additional Use Grant): shipping something that
  is not intended for commercial advantage or monetary compensation — a free hobby
  game, an academic project, a demo.

## Requires a commercial license

**Commercial production use** — shipping or operating a product that uses VaCuus for
commercial advantage or monetary compensation — before that version's Change Date.
Developing against the free copy and buying before release is an expected path, not a
loophole: the license you buy covers the release.

Two ways to get one:

1. **Fab** — buy the plugin on the store listing (link appears here when the listing is
   live); the Fab EULA's license covers commercial use of the purchased copy.
2. **Directly from the author** — <ufna@ufna.dev>, for teams that need terms Fab does
   not offer (custom support, source escrow, invoicing).

## The Change Date

Each released version of VaCuus converts to the **MIT license** four years after its
first public distribution. Old versions become plain open source on schedule; buying a
license is paying for the current four years, not forever.

## Third-party components

RmlUi 6.x and QuickJS-ng are vendored in-tree under their own MIT licenses
(`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`) in every channel and are never subject to the BUSL terms.
